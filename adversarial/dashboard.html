The defense fails under adaptive attack. 24.67% accuracy at ε=0.3 — worse than the undefended baseline's 48.99% at the same epsilon. The earlier "94.97%" was an artifact of testing against a stale attack, not real robustness.

5 Iterative Adversarial Training (5 Rounds)

Regenerating adversarial examples against the current model each round — the standard (Madry-style) approach — to see if repeated training converges to real robustness.

Training oscillates, never converges. Accuracy under adaptive attack bounces between 13.93% and 24.67% across 5 rounds — the model keeps re-fitting to whichever adversarial batch it last saw, without ever settling on a robust boundary.

6 Conclusion

Logistic Regression has a single linear decision boundary with a fixed margin. PGD only needs to push a point across that one boundary, and adversarial training can only shift/rotate that same hyperplane — it has no non-linear capacity to locally reshape the decision surface the way an MLP or tree ensemble could. This is a genuine, evaluation-honest negative result: naive adversarial training gives a false sense of security unless tested with adaptive attacks, and linear models have a fundamentally low ceiling for adversarial robustness under L∞ perturbations.
